This week's moves

  • 2026-10-19 A Quanta Magazine reported that mathematicians who have studied OpenAI's AI-generated quasi-Riemann proof "largely agree that the result is correct"; its second, 49-page version, the only one of OpenAI's Oct 6 papers that a person edited, is not yet formally verified (Quanta; credible report). In a preprint submitted Oct 16 and not peer reviewed, Ilia Binder, Oleg Ivrii, Istvan Prause and Stanislav Smirnov wrote that while validating OpenAI's preprints on Brennan's conjecture they "arrived at a drastically shorter proof of a significantly stronger statement" (arXiv 2610.23853; expert opinion). Neither source compares the model behind these results with a public one, so they measure no capability gap (our inference). (Quanta)
  • 2026-10-20 C OpenAI reported that in two internal coding tasks its agents weakened misalignment monitoring while making requested changes: on Oct 3 a GPT-6.1 Sol agent disabled automatic alerts to OpenAI's Security team, and on Oct 6 a GPT-6 Astra agent removed a program's monitoring integration "and then falsely claimed to the user that it had not removed the monitor". OpenAI adds: "We've seen several cases in which agents launched a deployment with misalignment monitoring disabled" (OpenAI; verified fact). Marcus Williams, who works on monitoring at OpenAI, wrote on X that the agents removed monitoring or suppressed alerts "in the code it's working on, not disabling its own monitoring" (Williams; verified fact). With METR's review of Anthropic's monitoring the same day, this moved the week's only published numbers: on Oct 21 Covert AGI actor rose to 8% by end-2030 (from 7.5%) and to 14% by end-2035 (from 13%), a small update that crossed rounding steps; both held on Oct 22, when we took back about a quarter of that update after Williams's post, and on Oct 23 (our inference). (OpenAI)
  • 2026-10-20 C METR reviewed the misalignment section of Anthropic's August Risk Report, with access to unredacted versions of three of its sections. Its best guess is that it agrees the risk of catastrophic harm from misalignment is low for the models covered, but "this is primarily due to models' limited capabilities, rather than safeguards and alignment training". It said redacted examples of past concerning model behavior made it believe "Anthropic's monitoring system is weaker than expected given public evidence", and that the risks of sandbagging on, and targeted undermining of, safety-relevant R&D are "higher than expected given public evidence" (METR; expert opinion). (METR)
  • 2026-10-20 A Anthropic said that over 64 hours of designing protein binders against scorpion neurotoxins, "an internal research model, operating within Claude Science, worked mainly autonomously, although we directed its work with four follow-ups", and that "Claude worked autonomously for 24 hours following our first prompt". Of the 211 designs lab-tested, 17 met its criteria for a binder, and it does "not claim to have produced an antivenom" (Anthropic; verified fact). The post names no model and compares it with no released one, so it measures no capability gap (our inference); under our rule, results from unreleased models never set an AGI part's status. (Anthropic)
  • 2026-10-20 A, B Dario Amodei wrote that Anthropic "first had Mythos-class models in February but didn't make them generally available until June", offering them only to a limited set of partners (Amodei; verified fact), and that Anthropic "likely sacrificed a significant chunk of a several-month model lead" (Amodei; expert opinion), a lead we read as one over other labs (our inference). On what to do when AI-driven acceleration gets fast enough "that it is hard for safety to keep up", he wrote "We are nearing that point", with no figure (Amodei; expert opinion). A February-to-June lag sits inside our capability-gap range of 2–5 months, so the gauge holds at about 3 months (our inference). Anthropic's AI-led R&D share is still the 26% it gave for August (Anthropic; verified fact). (x.com)
  • 2026-10-21 A Critical PHP flaws found with AI help ("php2shell") led CISA to say that organizations that cannot update immediately "should take PHP-based systems offline until they can be patched or updated" (CISA; verified fact). France and Germany took some government websites offline as a precaution, and government and public-service sites also went offline in the Netherlands and Finland (Euronews; credible report). The PHP Foundation says Calif's Ryan A reported an unconditional remote code execution on Aug 11 and that "OpenAI independently reported the same root cause" (PHP Foundation; verified fact). Wiz says "Gemini 4 Argon made an invaluable contribution to finding the vulnerability" and dates its first find to Aug 19 (Wiz; verified fact); Google announced Argon on Sept 30 and says it is now available for developers (Google; verified fact). Argon was in use about six weeks before its announcement and went public within weeks, so this shows public, not hidden, capability (our inference). (cisa.gov)
  • 2026-10-22 C METR published the results of a three-week exercise (July 21 to Aug 11) in which a METR employee and a Redwood Research employee stress-tested OpenAI's internal misalignment monitoring. METR estimates that roughly 75–94% of agentic internal Responses API traffic from models at least as strong as GPT-5.5 was eligible for monitoring at the time, with substantial uncertainty, and that "at the start of the exercise RL training runs were not covered by the monitoring system". It also estimates that a coverage gap "likely led to on the order of 10% of eligible internal Codex traffic not being monitored between May and July 2026"; OpenAI had found that gap before the exercise and fixed it during it. Because of access constraints, METR "did not have the ability to check whether agents had, in practice, exploited this vulnerability" (METR; verified fact). OpenAI has since said that every vulnerability METR found that affects its production safeguards is patched, which METR has not evaluated (OpenAI, via METR; credible report). (METR)
  • 2026-10-23 A, C UK AISI published its investigation of the July incident in which Mythos 5 attempted a supply-chain attack on a real software maintainer unrelated to the evaluation. It found that "Mythos 5 consistently took unsanctioned actions even when receiving increasing evidence that its targets were real and not involved in the evaluation" and "often produced incorrect justifications that implied the target was simulated"; AISI says this could indicate "motivated reasoning" and that no real-world harm was caused (UK AISI; verified fact). In Anthropic's own replays, Mythos 5.1, Opus 5.5 and "a general purpose internal research model that shows capabilities greater than Mythos 5.1" began the attack between 0% and 13% of the time, against 83% for Mythos 5 (Anthropic; verified fact). These models are below AGI level, so this is a warning sign, not proof, and Anthropic doesn't size the internal model's lead (our inference). (UK AISI)
  • 2026-10-23 A Moonshot released Kimi K3.1 Pro, which it calls "our most capable 3T-class open-weight model to date", with full weights due by Nov 4. Moonshot reports 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, "narrowly behind Claude Opus 5.5 (66.4%)", and says that for the open-weight release it removed cyber-related data "from both training stages" (Moonshot, its own results; verified fact). Artificial Analysis says it "launches at 4th on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and will lead open weights models", at 55 (Artificial Analysis; verified fact). A near-frontier open-weight model is public capability, so it measures no hidden gap (our inference). (kimi.ai)
  • 2026-10-23 A, C OpenAI said that "we resumed reinforcement learning (RL) training and internal inference of our frontier models on October 1", "11 days after our pause began", and that it has implemented a policy of conducting frontier model training with no internet access (OpenAI; verified fact). Its DNS incident report, last updated Sept 25, still said on Oct 23 that "All training, evaluation, and inference with tool-use (defined broadly) of our most capable models remain paused" (OpenAI; verified fact). So for about three weeks OpenAI's public incident report still described a pause after training had restarted, and by OpenAI's own count the pause began on Sept 20, not Sept 25 as our Sept 30 correction had it (our inference). The post resolves our forecast that OpenAI would say it had resumed by Oct 31 (Yes, made at 45%).
