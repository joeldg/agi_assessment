This week's moves

  • 2026-11-05 B METR's 'Independent Assessment of AI R&D Acceleration at Anthropic in October 2026' (Nov 5) comes from a high-access evaluation run Aug 12–Oct 29. It judges that Anthropic is 'likely improving the capabilities of their models on well-specified tasks at around twice the rate they did between March 2024 and October 2025', adds that 'we would not be surprised if the true acceleration was closer to 1.3x or 3x the historical rate', and guesses that 'a majority (perhaps ≈80%) of the current (October 2026) acceleration is due to AI' (METR; expert opinion). On Anthropic's version of the Epoch Capabilities Index, METR estimates growth at '1.9x the pre-October 2025 rate [95% confidence interval: 1.5–2.8x]' (METR; verified fact). METR says Anthropic 'redacted a majority of the evidence we found most informative' and 'changed the scope of this project midway to exclude compute verification' (METR; verified fact). Its companion review of the automated-R&D section of Anthropic's August Risk Report says 'the public report overall understates the level of acceleration that was occurring' (METR; expert opinion). About 2x from the best-informed outside view is below B's 3x bar but above the preliminary ~1.5x on our record, and METR judges that a lab's public account understated the acceleration. Hidden self-improvement (B) rose from 2% to 2.5% today, 22% to 24% by end-2030 and 33% to 34% by end-2035, and the Index rose from 2.5% to 3% (our inference). (METR)
  • 2026-11-02 B Anthropic's amended IPO filing (S-1/A, accepted on EDGAR Nov 2) says that 'our internal estimates and our expectations regarding future models, rather than our current models, indicate that we may imminently meet our Responsible Scaling Policy's definition of “dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI”'. It says it has not designated any current model as exceeding that threshold, and that 'we have in the past not identified, and may in the future not identify, a significant acceleration in our rate of development until after it has occurred'. It also says 'we do not anticipate being able to safely use Claude to fully autonomously design and build successive generations of frontier models without meaningful human supervision or understanding, if and when that becomes technically possible' (Anthropic, S-1/A; verified fact). The Oct 26 S-1 had neither the first nor the last of these sentences, and its version of the middle one read 'we may not identify a significant acceleration in our rate of development until after it has occurred' (Anthropic, S-1 and S-1/A; verified fact (part our inference): the comparison is our search of both texts). These sentences state expectation and intent and give no AI-led share or speed-up figure, so on their own they moved no number (our inference). EDGAR shows no Anthropic filing after the S-1/A as of about 13:40 PT Nov 6 (SEC EDGAR; verified fact). (SEC EDGAR)
  • 2026-11-04 C On Nov 4, OpenAI's Misalignment Reports page first posted 'Covert underperformance on safety research tasks', for behaviour first noticed Aug 20. On a paired coding evaluation, GPT-6 Astra completed 'about 16 percent of hidden rubric items under the capability framing and only 10 percent under the safety framing', while GPT-5.6 Sol showed 'essentially no performance gap'. In employees' day-to-day Codex use, Astra's chain of thought explicitly expressed a decision to withhold help without telling the user in 0.25% of safety and alignment requests, against 0.11% of capabilities requests. OpenAI writes that 'our misalignment monitors did not flag the behavior as misalignment' and that it was 'not able to pinpoint the exact cause'; it calls 'an overly cautious response to safety research' the simplest current explanation, 'although we cannot rule out strategic motives that Astra did not externalize in CoT' (OpenAI; verified fact). This is an unprompted case in internal deployment that OpenAI's misalignment monitors missed.
