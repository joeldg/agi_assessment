# Hidden AGI watch

A daily, sourced assessment (aiming for calibration; the track record is on the [scorecard](https://hiddenagi.com/scorecard.html)) of four hypotheses about advanced AI, published with GitHub Pages at <https://hiddenagi.com/>:

- **A · Hidden AGI**: a system meets AGI as we define it, and whoever built it, a company or a government program, has kept that level of capability from the public for at least 30 days. Admitting that an unreleased model exists is not disclosure.
- **B · Hidden self-improvement**: an AI system does most of the work of building a more capable successor, with at least a 3x speed-up over humans alone, and whoever runs it, a company or a government program, has kept this from the public for at least 30 days. Partial AI-driven acceleration is already public, so it doesn't count. B does not need AGI.
- **C · Covert AGI actor**: an AGI-level system takes sustained actions online or in the economy, and the public hasn't known for at least 30 days: sanctioned but undisclosed (its developer or another operator runs it on purpose and doesn't say so), or rogue or stolen (it acts outside its developer's control).
- **D · Covert government influence**: output from an AGI-level system materially shapes a major government decision without the public knowing. **D-open · Open government influence** is the same through acknowledged government use, even of a system whose AGI-level capability hasn't been disclosed; it sits outside the Hidden AGI Index, which counts only hidden scenarios.

These are short labels. The exact wording of A–D is on [Could it be hidden?](https://hiddenagi.com/hidden.html#hypotheses), and what we mean by AGI is on [Is AGI here?](https://hiddenagi.com/agi.html#definition). Changes to either are dated in `data/method.json` and listed on [Changes and corrections](https://hiddenagi.com/changes.html#method).

**What we mean by AGI (definitions v2.0).** A system with the full scope of human cognitive autonomy: it can learn, reason, plan and generalize knowledge across any intellectual domain, without task-specific retraining. Physical and embodied work is out of scope. We track the definition in eight parts, each with a published test: breadth of work, professional quality, reliability, reasoning, planning and long projects, autonomy without scaffolding, learning on the job, and generalization to the new. A system counts as AGI only when it meets all eight parts' bars at the same time. The v1.0 remote-work test (reliably doing at least 80% of economically valuable remote professional tasks at the level of a median skilled professional, including multi-week projects) stays as one of the tests. Definitions v2.0 took effect with the method entry of 5 Oct 2026, so the reading of 6 Oct 2026 is the first under v2.0. Readings and forecasts made on or before 5 Oct 2026 keep their meaning and resolve under definitions v1.0, the strict remote-work bar.

A custom AI agent updates the site once a day. Each run searches current news, research and evaluations, reassesses the hypotheses, and adds a news roundup. It publishes automatically. The daily and weekly email issues are sent automatically; breaking fire-alarm alerts are approved by a person before they are sent. The owner sets the definitions and the alarm thresholds.

## Setup

Python 3.9 or newer (the scheduled tasks use the system `python3`). The scripts use only the standard library, except `scripts/update_trends.py`, which needs PyYAML:

```sh
python3 -m pip install --user -r requirements.txt
```

`scripts/render_card.py` and `scripts/smoke.py` need Google Chrome in /Applications. `scripts/update_sec.py` sends a contact address in its User-Agent, as the SEC requires, taken from `$SEC_CONTACT_EMAIL` or `git config user.email`; it is never written to disk. `scripts/kit_broadcast.py` needs `KIT_API_KEY` or `KIT_API_SECRET` in the environment.

Tests (stdlib `unittest`, read-only, a few seconds): `python3 -m unittest discover -s scripts/tests -t .`

## Data fields

- **`data/runs.json`**, one entry per reading:
  - `date`, `label`, `report`, and `comparable` (false for the quick chat baseline, which is kept for the record but never used for change figures) with a `note`.
  - `probs`: A, B, C, D, Dopen → `now` / `y2030` / `y2035` / `conf`. `agi` ("AGI anywhere": some system, public or hidden, meets AGI as defined in the definitions in force) has the same fields, plus `k` from the first v2.0 reading on (`{now, y2030, y2035}`: our probability that a system meeting the v1.0 remote-work test by then also meets the rest of definitions v2.0; set from the method entry, then moved only on evidence about the added parts). On readings before definitions v2.0, `agi` is the v1.0 strict bar. The horizons are cumulative: `y2030` means true at any point before 1 Jan 2031.
  - `index` and `indexNote`: the Hidden AGI Index, the probability that at least one of A–D is true now.
  - `needle`: `quiet`, `hypothesis`, `from`, `to`, `subject` (the email subject hook: at most 50 characters, plain words, no hypothesis letters), `headline`, `detail`, `url`.
  - `gauges`: `gap`, `rd`, `oversight`, `money`, `delegation` → `value`, `display`, `note`, `url`, optional `range`.
  - `alarm`: that day's `level`, `met` conditions and `criteria` version.
  - `tripwires`: `id`, `hyp`, `signal`, `status` (stored as quiet, watching or tripped; shown as Quiet, Open or Confirmed), `since`, `note`, `url`, and `trigger`, the alarm condition it feeds where there is one.
  - `escape`: the counts `watching`, `tripped`, `quiet` and a `note`.
  - `roundup`: `[{topic, items: [{date, text, url, top}]}]`, where `top: true` marks the items the email shows, plus `roundupWindow`.
  - `corrections`: `[{date, page, was, now, url}]`, the corrections from `data/corrections.json` that this day's email carries. `date` is when the correction was made; the email dates the original claim from `page`.
  - `summary`, `changes`, `timeline`, `signals`, `sources`.
  - **Format 2** (every reading from 6 Oct 2026; earlier entries are never edited to add these):
    - `format: 2` (rendered by `build_report.py`), `defs` (the definitions in force on the reading's date, "2.0"), `analysis` (`reports/<date>-analysis.html`).
    - Text for the short report: `verdict` (30 words or fewer; the h1), `needle.brief` (100 words or fewer; adds detail, never restates the verdict), `needle.rating`, `needle.url` and `needle.source` (the rating chip and source link), `timelineShort` (40 words or fewer), `bottomLine` (60 words or fewer), `roundup[].items[].short` (25 words or fewer) and `.rating` on the 3–6 top items, and `gauges.<key>.brief` (20 words or fewer) only for a gauge that moved.
    - Decisions: `tripwireChanges` (`[{id, from, to, why}]`), `escape.changes` (`[{key, from, to, reason}]`) and `escape.newEvidence` (indicator keys), `indexParts` (`[{key: "A" | "B" | "CD", v}]`, each to the nearest 0.05, whose sum rounds to `index` on our grid; when the sum is lower, the Index bar shows the difference as a rounding segment), optional `secrecyNow` (the chance of being kept quiet 30+ days used for A), `componentLeads` (`[{id, claim, url, rating, why}]`: verified results that might move an AGI part, for the weekly to check), and `methodChange` (`{from, to, note}`, only on the first reading after a definitions entry).
    - Snapshots, copied by `build_report.py --snapshot DATE` so the report never changes later: `components` (`{<part id>: {status, short, glance, basisShort}}` for the eight parts), `furthest` (the part furthest behind: `{id, short, display, targetDisplay}`), `alarm.since`, `.icon`, `.status`, `.name`, and `dates` (the next three calendar events, `[{date, short}]`).
- **`data/agi_components.json`** (new with definitions v2.0): `definitionsVersion`, `adopted` (the method entry's date), `lastReviewed`, `definition` (`text`, `excludes`, `remoteWorkTest`, `rule`), `hypotheses` (A, B, C, D, Dopen → `name`, `text`: the one public wording), `statusScale` (far, partial, close, met → `word`, `pips`, `meaning`), `statusRule`, optional `publicAgi`, `components` (exactly eight, in order: breadth, quality, reliability, reasoning, horizon, autonomy, learning, generalization), `frameworks` (outside definitions, with `best`, optional `bestUrl`), `frameworksSource` and `history` (`[{date, id, from, to, why, url}]`, append-only, one row per status change). Each part has `id`, `order`, `name`, `short`, `v1Test`, `clause`, `question`, `status`, `since`, `statusBasis` and `basisShort` (why it has that status), `lastReviewed`, `glance`, `statusNote`, `statusDetail`, `bar`, `test` (`measure`, `threshold` with its Partial, Close and Met marks, `detail`, `why`), `parts` (`[{name, state: pass | aggregate | no | unmeasured, value}]`), `headline` (the binding measure against the bar, with its marks, scale, `asOf`, `url` and `rating`), `also`, `sharedWith`, `current` (latest measurements), `milestones` (dated, with `kind`: result, internal or yardstick), `projection` (with an optional `trend` read from `data/trends.json`), `upcoming`, `challenges`, `who`, `watching` (`{signal, threshold, sourceName, url, sourceDetail, why}`) and `hiddenAngle`. Items carry `rating`, and optionally `ratingNote` and `ratingQual` (see Evidence ratings). The definition, hypotheses, scale, rule, each part's test, bar, part names and headline marks change only with a method change; the weekly maintains the rest.
- **`data/forecasts.json`**: `id`, `question`, `p`, `made`, `deadline`, `resolution`, `context`, `url`; `market` with `marketAsOf`, `marketSource` and `marketUrl`; optional `tests` (the gauges or alarm conditions a forecast is a proxy for, e.g. `["W2", "gap"]`); `corrections` (`[{date, text, url}]`); `original_context` and `original_url`, the reasoning and source as made, kept when a correction rewrites `context` (the scorecard shows both); `outcome` (true, false, or `"void"` with `voidReason` for a withdrawn forecast) and `resolved`.
- **`data/incidents.json`**: `occurred` (with `occurredApprox`, and the window in `occurredFrom` / `occurredTo` when a date is approximate), `detected` and `detectedBy` (who noticed first), `disclosed`, `foundBy` (who first made it public), `lab`, `note`, `url`, `press`. The file's note gives the inclusion rule.
- **`data/external_forecasts.json`**: `who`, `what`, `date`, `p`, `asOf`, `definition`, `rating`, `url`, plus `bar` (announcement, narrower, stricter, different or own-definition) and `gap`, a line on how it differs from definitions v2.0. `barV1` and `gapV1` keep the comparison with the v1.0 strict bar, and the file's `noteV1` its earlier note.
- **`data/agi_claims.json`**: `definitions` (the bars each claim is judged against; `v2` is definitions v2.0, `strict` the v1.0 bar) and `claims`, each with a `meets` verdict per bar (`meets.v2` for definitions v2.0).
- **`data/corrections.json`**: `corrections: [{date, page, item, was, now, url, emailed}]`. `emailed` is null until a daily email carries the correction.
- **`data/method.json`**: `version` (the highest entry's version) and `changelog` (`[{version, date, change, why}]`, append-only). A definitions entry also has `definitions` (e.g. "2.0"), `definitionText` (equal to `data/agi_components.json` `definition.text`), `factors` (`{now, y2030, y2035}`, each in (0, 1]) and `rule` (how the next reading re-derives the numbers). **The definitions in force for a reading dated D** are those of the last entry with `definitions` dated before D, or "1.0" if there is none: the morning reading on the entry's own date stays on the old definitions, and the next reading is the first under the new ones.
- **`data/alarm.json`** (criteria v1.2): `version`, `published`, `purpose`, `levels`, `evidenceStandard`, `exitRule` (`{"mode": "rule-unsatisfied", "days": 30}`), `rules`, `proofStandard` (the Level-3 proof standard: `summary`, `requirements`, `modalities`, `tribunal`, `otherwise`), `caseFile` (the alarm case-file standard: `verdict`, `sections`, `correction`, `appendix`, `files`, `register`, `fields`), `cases` (the case register: `id`, `trigger`, `status`, `opened`, `decided`, `file`, `page`), `groups` (each condition has `id`, `trigger`, `threshold`, `why`, `clears`, `met`, `since`, `evidence`, `url`, `short` (a label of 12 words or fewer for the one-line rows, display only), and optionally `press` (corroborating coverage), `borderline`, `observable`, `watchFrom`), `current`, `history` (`date`, `from`, `to`, `cause`, `triggers`, `cleared`, `note`, `reviewDue`, `review`) and `changelog`.
- **`data/escape.json`**: `intro`, `overall` (today's summary), `limits` (what it can't see), `updated`, `statusLegend` (meanings for Quiet, Open and Confirmed), and `indicators`: `key`, `name`, `status` (stored as quiet, watching or tripped; shown as Quiet, Open or Confirmed), `statusReason`, optional `short` (20 words or fewer, the line shown on the page; otherwise its first sentence), `whatItWouldLookLike`, `evidence` (`[{date, text, url, rating}]`), `feeds` (`[{name, url, cadence}]`), `alarmTriggers` (main one first), `alarmNote` and `tripRule` (shown as the confirm rule).
- **`data/calendar.json`**: events may carry an optional `short` title (14 words or fewer), which the reports' dates list uses.
- **`data/weekly/YYYY-MM-DD.json`**, from the first wrap-up after the redesign: the snapshot adds `labelSet: 2`, `agi` (each part's status, name and glance as of that Friday) and `breaks` (where the definitions changed, for the trend chart); `keyNumbers` adds `agiParts` (counts by status), `agi`, `defs`, `defsWeekAgo`, `methodBoundary`, `methodChangeDate` and `news` (moves on news inside a week that also had a method change).

## Policies

- **Frozen once published.** Past readings, past reports and their analysis pages, made forecasts (`p`, `question`, `resolution`, `made`, `deadline`) and the append-only logs (incidents, steelman, corrections, the method changelog, the AGI parts' status history, alarm history and changelog) are never edited. `scripts/check_data.py` enforces this against git. `build_report.py` refuses to re-render a past date.
- **Corrections are public.** A wrong claim gets a dated entry in `data/corrections.json`, a dated "Correction" note on the page that carried the claim (usually the analysis page, and the short report too if it repeated the claim) and a correction box in the next daily email. A wrong forecast premise gets a dated entry in that forecast's `corrections`; it is still scored as made. An ill-posed forecast can be withdrawn unscored (`outcome: "void"` with `voidReason`); the scorecard always shows the reason.
- **Evidence ratings.** Every factual claim links its source and carries one of six ratings: verified fact, credible report, expert opinion, forecast aggregate, our inference, speculation. "Verified fact" means a primary source, or two independent credible outlets that were actually opened. Leaked documents, single anonymously sourced reports and "reportedly" items are "credible report". "Expert opinion" is a named expert's or organization's judgment or forecast. Our own inferences carry the "our inference" chip, or, inside a sentence that is otherwise verified fact, the qualifier "(part our inference)" after the fact chip; crowd and market figures carry "forecast aggregate". "Speculation" marks a possibility no evidence yet supports. In the data, `ratingQual` (5 words or fewer) is the qualifier shown after the chip, and `ratingNote` (a complete sentence) is the chip's title and names any inferred clause. Secondhand sources are marked "via". The legend is on `start-here.html#method`.
- **Market prices** come only from the Kalshi and Polymarket public APIs, with the date and bid/ask, never from press coverage.
- **Definitions and alarm criteria** are the owner's call. They change only with a dated, versioned changelog entry, and never while an alarm-level change is being considered.
- **Definitions v2.0** (method entry of 5 Oct 2026) replace the v1.0 strict AGI bar wherever we used it: the AGI-anywhere forecast, hypotheses A, C, D and D-open, and alarm condition Y1. Physical and embodied work is out of scope, and the v1.0 remote-work test stays as one of the tests. Readings and forecasts made under v1.0 keep their meaning and resolve under v1.0.
- **A definitions change is a method change, never news.** The first reading after a definitions entry re-derives every number that needs an AGI-level system by the entry's published rule, after first deciding the day on the old basis. It carries `methodChange`, every re-derived number gets a "Method change (definitions v2.0):" line in `changes`, the reports, the email, the card and the weekly show "method change" instead of up or down arrows for those numbers, and the needle is never the definition change. A number that also moved on evidence that day keeps its own news line ("news; then method change"). A withdrawal of a definition is one more definitions entry, handled the same way.
- **AGI parts.** The daily reading never edits `data/agi_components.json`; it records verified results that might move a part in `componentLeads`. Only the weekly AGI parts lens changes a status, by the published rule (`statusRule`): a part's status is the highest mark in its `test.threshold` that the best public evidence reaches; a part passed on the overall figure only does not count as passed; results from unreleased models never set a status. A status change gets a `history` row. `publicAgi` (a public system meeting all eight parts) is set only after the owner confirms it.
- **Alarm criteria v1.2** (5 Oct 2026). Y1 is restated on definitions v2.0: an undisclosed system must meet AGI as defined on `agi.html`, on evidence equivalent to passing all eight parts' tests at once, including both halves of the earlier remote-work test. Y2 refers to Y1's bar and follows it. Each condition also has a short display label. No other condition, threshold, level or rule changed. A level rises the day its rule is satisfied and drops only after its rule has gone unsatisfied for 30 consecutive days, to the level the rules then give. Borderline values don't count. Every condition says when it clears. The full list of changes is in the alarm changelog and on `changes.html#criteria`.
- **The Level-3 proof standard.** Alarm (Level 3) needs convergent proof beyond reasonable doubt, not official confirmation, so it can fire before a developer admits anything: at least three independent lines of evidence from different modalities that share no original source; every fact authenticated and sourced; no single innocent explanation that accounts for all the lines, each one examined in writing; and a unanimous adversarial tribunal (prosecution, defense, an authenticity check and independent judges). Until a case passes, the level can't go above Warning, and the case is shown as under investigation. A case is published in the alarm case-file standard, fixed in advance on `alarm.html`: a one-sentence verdict, then what we claim and don't, the evidence lines, every innocent explanation considered, what could have disproved it, what we still don't know, what readers can do, and a public-correction commitment, with a full evidence appendix page.
- **One word, one meaning.** "Level" is the fire alarm's word only (Level 0–3); the delegation gauge is read in rungs. Signals (tripwires and Escape watch indicators) are Quiet, Open or Confirmed. AGI parts are Far, Partial, Close or Met. "AGI anywhere" is our probability that AGI exists, public or hidden; "Index" alone means the Hidden AGI Index, and outside indexes are named in full. Data field names, ids and stored status values never change; only the display words do.
- **Report pages.** Nobody writes report HTML by hand: the daily writes its reading into `data/runs.json` and the analysis body, and `build_report.py` renders both pages. Every report page, the analysis page included, has two subscribe boxes, one after the header and one before the footer.
- **Voice.** We write in the first person plural, use plain verbs and call the author "a custom AI agent".
- **Publishing.** Each run stops if the working tree has uncommitted changes in the files it reads or writes, commits only the paths it names (never `git add -A` or a force-push), checks after staging that nothing it produced is left uncommitted, and pushes only after `scripts/check_data.py` passes.
- <a id="sending"></a>**Sending.** The daily issue (10am Pacific) and the Friday wrap-up (3pm Pacific) are sent automatically. If a target time has passed, the issue is sent as soon as possible rather than left as a draft, and an issue is never held back because the alarm level changed: it goes out showing the new level. The daily issue goes out only once the report, its analysis page and the card are live. A breaking fire-alarm alert is always created as a draft, and a person approves it before it's sent.

## Daily update procedure

The daily run starts at about 09:02 Pacific; the email goes at 10am, or as soon as the reading is published if that is later.

1. **Start clean.** Fetch, and stop if the working tree has uncommitted changes in the files the run reads or writes (everything under `data/`, `reports/`, `cards/` and `scripts/`, `feed.xml`, `sitemap.xml` and every generated page). Run `python3 scripts/check_data.py` on the untouched tree. If its only errors are about `data/agi_components.json`, restore the last committed version that passes, carry on and tell the owner; any other failure stops the run.
2. **Definitions in force.** `python3 scripts/defs_status.py --date <today>` says which definitions are in force, what the previous published reading used, and the mode: "steady", or "method-change" on the first reading after a definitions entry.
3. **The reading.** Read `data/runs.json`, the previous published reading (its short report and analysis page), `data/method.json`, `data/agi_components.json` (read-only), open corrections and reported issues; research the news since the last reading, and verify every material claim before using it. Run `python3 scripts/alarm_check.py`, re-check the alarm conditions (`data/alarm.json`) and the Escape watch indicators (`data/escape.json`), then decide the Index and its parts, the probabilities, the needle (or a quiet day), the five gauges, each signal's status and any `componentLeads`. Level 3 can be set only when a case passes under the Level-3 proof standard; otherwise it holds at Warning, under investigation. On a method-change day, decide on the old basis first, then apply the method entry's rule (see Policies).
4. **Write.** Add the day's format-2 entry to `data/runs.json` (a rerun replaces the same day's entry), then `python3 scripts/build_report.py --snapshot <today>` to copy the snapshots. Write the analysis body (the sections `alarm`, `index`, `gauges`, `tripwires`, `escape`, `agi`, `timeline`, `roundup`, `s1`–`s6`, `bottom`, in that order; allowed HTML only) and render both pages with `python3 scripts/build_report.py --date <today> --body <file>`. A data problem exits 2 naming the field; the fix goes in the data or the body, never in the HTML.
5. **Validate and build, in order:** `python3 scripts/alarm_check.py` (exit 0), `python3 scripts/render_card.py --date <today>`, `python3 scripts/build_report.py --date <today> --check` (exit 0), `python3 scripts/build_pages.py`, `python3 scripts/build_feed.py`, `python3 scripts/smoke.py --anchors-only --published-warn` (every link and fragment resolves), `python3 scripts/check_data.py` (must pass) and `python3 scripts/check_data.py --pages` (word budgets, warnings only). Any later edit to today's entry needs `build_report.py --date <today>` again before `check_data.py`. The daily never runs `build_money.py` and never changes `data/agi_components.json`.
6. **Commit** the named paths (the report, the analysis page, `data/runs.json`, `data/alarm.json`, the cards, the generated pages, `feed.xml`, `sitemap.xml`, and when changed `data/escape.json`, `data/corrections.json` and corrected past pages), check that nothing the run produced is left uncommitted, and push to `main`.
7. **Send.** Wait until the report, the analysis page and the card are live, then run `python3 scripts/kit_broadcast.py --date <today> --send-at 10am` (via `zsh -ic` so the keys load) and commit `data/kit_broadcasts.json`. Drafts and failures are flagged to the owner.
8. **Fire-alarm follow-ups.** If the alarm level changed, `kit_broadcast.py --alarm` drafts a breaking alert for a person to approve. If a Y condition may be met, the alarm-case tribunal runs; if it passes and the level changes, today's entry is updated and both report pages are re-rendered (`--snapshot`, then `--date`, then `--check`) before the rebuild and push.

The first reading under definitions v2.0 is due on 6 Oct 2026, and it is also the first in format 2: `defs_status.py` gives "method-change" that day, and the reading compares against the single legacy page of 5 Oct 2026.

## Weekly wrap-up (Fridays: built about 1pm, emailed at 3pm Pacific)

1. Work out the wrap-up date (the most recent Friday), and stop on a duplicate. A Saturday catch-up is left as a draft; a later one is skipped. Wait for the Friday daily reading if it's still running. Then the same clean-tree check as the daily.
2. Update `data/forecasts.json` (resolve due forecasts; add new ones, at least one a proxy for a gauge or alarm condition; never edit a made forecast), `data/incidents.json`, `data/agi_claims.json` (including `meets.v2` for new claims), `data/calendar.json`, `data/external_forecasts.json` (`bar` and `gap` against definitions v2.0), `data/escape.json` (indicator `short` lines); run `python3 scripts/update_trends.py`; run `python3 scripts/update_sec.py`, add the other money figures to `data/money_sources.json`, then run `python3 scripts/build_money.py`; add a new entry to `data/steelman.json`.
3. **AGI parts.** Re-open each part's sources and look for newer published results, milestones, dated events and people, and check the week's `componentLeads`. Every proposed change is checked by a second pass that tries to refute it before it is applied to `data/agi_components.json`. A status changes only when the test's own mark is reached by verified public evidence, with a `history` row. The fields that define the test are never edited. A part that reaches Met goes to the owner first.
4. Write `data/weekly/<date>.json` (headline, subject, summary, moves, section notes including the AGI parts, and any gauge updates for the next daily). The wrap-up never edits a published daily reading or report. If definitions changed during the week, the summary names it once, as a method change with a link to `changes.html#method`, and no re-derived number appears among the week's moves, in the headline or in the subject.
5. **Jobs.** Run `python3 scripts/import_jobs.py --date <date>`: exit 0 imports the plugin's newest edition (dated in the seven days to the Friday) into `data/jobs/`; exit 3 means no plugin or no edition this week, and the wrap-up goes out without Jobs; exit 2 means the edition was refused (the reason is printed): carry on without Jobs and tell the owner.
6. Run `python3 scripts/build_weekly.py <date>`, `python3 scripts/build_pages.py`, `python3 scripts/build_money.py`, `python3 scripts/build_feed.py`, `python3 scripts/check_data.py` and `python3 scripts/smoke.py --widths 375,1280 --anchors --published-warn`. Commit the named paths (including `data/agi_components.json` and the generated pages when changed) and push. Anything not committed by 08:30 Pacific on Saturday is restored, so the Saturday daily starts clean. When `import_jobs.py` imported an edition, the named paths also include `data/jobs/<date>.json`, `data/jobs/claims.json` and `jobs.html` (and `data/corrections.json` when it gained a Jobs correction): the email links `jobs.html`, and `kit_broadcast.py` waits until it is live.
7. Run `python3 scripts/kit_broadcast.py --weekly <date> --send-at 3pm`.

The wrap-up page shows the key numbers in the order they build on each other (AGI parts, AGI anywhere, the Index, the fire alarm, then the rest), an AGI parts section, "method change (definitions v2.0)" instead of arrows in a week that spans a definitions change, and the week's corrections as a count and titles linking `changes.html#corrections`.

## Jobs plugin

The Friday wrap-up ends with a "Jobs" section when the [Jobs plugin](https://github.com/joeldg/post_agi_work) has an edition for the week: a weekly thesis tracker on the move to a post-AGI economy, with thirteen testable claims (J0, the null, to J12), each with written marks for its status. It never feeds the Index, the odds, the gauges or the fire alarm.

- **The contract.** The plugin lives in its own repo (`../post_agi_work`, or `--plugin DIR`, or `$JOBS_PLUGIN`) and runs on Thursdays. `scripts/import_jobs.py --date <FRIDAY>` reads only its committed `HEAD`, takes the newest `editions/<date>.json` dated in the seven days to the Friday, validates it with `scripts/checks/jobs.py` and this repo's denylist, and writes `data/jobs/<FRIDAY>.json` and `data/jobs/claims.json`. Any corrections it carries are appended once to `data/corrections.json` with `"section": "jobs"`, so they reach `changes.html` and the next daily email.
- **Exit codes.** 0 imported; 3 skipped (no plugin, or no edition this week: the wrap-up is exactly as it would be without Jobs); 2 refused (nothing written; the reason is printed).
- **What readers see.** The wrap-up page's last section and an email block of at most 200 words (`scripts/jobs_render.py`), and `jobs.html`, "Jobs watch". Published `data/jobs/<date>.json` files are frozen like every other published file.
