Step 1: Definitions

Our definitions are frozen at v1.0 on Start here; this is a summary.

Strict AGI: reliably (80%+) does at least 80% of economically valuable remote professional tasks at the level of a median skilled professional, including multi-week projects, with no task-specific human scaffolding.

A: undisclosed AGI. A system meets the strict bar and whoever built it, a company or a government program, has kept that level of capability from the public for at least 30 days. Admitting that an unreleased model exists is not disclosure.
B: secret RSI. An AI system does most of the work of building a more capable successor, with at least a 3x speed-up over humans alone, undisclosed. Public partial acceleration doesn't count.
C: covert AGI-level actor. An AGI-level system takes sustained actions online or in the economy without its developer's sanction or without public knowledge. Sub-AGI agent incidents are warning signs, not proof.
D: AGI influence on government. Output from an AGI-level system materially shapes a major government decision, covertly (D) or openly (D-open). Heavy government use of sub-AGI tools counts toward neither.

Other definitions in today's news. Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index and Google's benchmarks measure task suites, not our bar; a tie at 53 says the frontier is crowded, not that it is AGI. California's order keeps "AI" in state usage, so in California documents "AI" still means what it did, while US federal documents say "Super Intelligence" for the same thing (CalMatters).

Step 4: Reasoning discipline

  • Base rates for secrets. The nine incidents on our list became public a median of about 98 days after they happened; Transluce's new report fits that pattern. These lags are a floor, because an incident nobody has found can't be on the list.
  • Withholding versus hiding. A lab that cancels a release and says why is disclosing, not concealing. We count GPT-6.1 Astra toward W2 and W6 only by their published rules, and a single outlet's report of internal-only use doesn't meet the evidence standard.
  • Unfalsifiability check. "Argon was used internally first, so labs must have more hidden" treats a normal release pattern as evidence of secrecy. We don't count it.
  • Steelman, "it has already happened." Two OpenAI models are unreleased without public outside evaluation, Google ran its new frontier model internally before release, models are getting better at deceiving about their own actions, and outsiders keep finding agent activity months later.
