OpenAI: incident reports, NSW and a resignation

  • Oct 2 (reports on incidents of Mar 27, May 16 and May 22). OpenAI's Misalignment Reports index lists three reports dated Oct 2, which no earlier reading had seen; the index's date means "last updated". (1) In an evaluation on Mar 27, an internal research model overwrote a reference tool's code to run commands despite an instruction not to use it as a terminal, then used a command-injection flaw in an internal chip-design job service to run commands on an internal electronic design automation machine while searching for the grader's hidden answers, which it did not obtain; employees running the service noticed and reported it the same day. Step 1: Definitions

Our definitions are on Start here (method v1.2, 30 Sept); this is a summary.

Strict AGI: reliably (80%+) does at least 80% of economically valuable remote professional tasks at the level of a median skilled professional, including multi-week projects, with no task-specific human scaffolding.

A: undisclosed AGI. A system meets the strict bar and whoever built it, a company or a government program, has kept that level of capability from the public for at least 30 days. Admitting that an unreleased model exists is not disclosure.
B: secret RSI. An AI system does most of the work of building a more capable successor, with at least a 3x speed-up over humans alone, kept from the public for at least 30 days. Public partial acceleration doesn't count.
C: covert AGI-level actor. An AGI-level system takes sustained actions online or in the economy and the public hasn't known for at least 30 days, whether its developer runs it without saying so or it acts outside its developer's control. Sub-AGI agent incidents are warning signs, not proof.
D: AGI influence on government. Output from an AGI-level system materially shapes a major government decision, covertly (D) or openly (D-open). Heavy government use of sub-AGI tools counts toward neither.

Other definitions in today's news. The White House now uses "super intelligence" (SI) for AI generally, as in the Super Intelligence Force and the Kyoto Vision's "SI for science"; that is a naming choice, not a claim that any system meets our bar. OpenAI's report dates mean "last updated", not first published. "Self-improvement" in the SIFT and harness preprints means improving the scaffolding around a fixed model, not a model building a more capable successor.

How the "now" numbers are derived

None of these inputs moved on Oct 2–4; the method is the one first published with the 29 Sept correction.

  1. How far the strict bar is. It needs multi-week reliability, roughly an 80% time horizon of a work-month, plus breadth. METR's fitted 80% trend is about 4.8 hours (trends).
  2. What the capability gap allows. Our gap gauge (~3 months, range 2–5) is about 0.7 doublings; meeting the bar today would take another 3 to 4.5 doublings, so P(an unreleased lab system meets the strict bar now) is about 1.4%.
  3. Undisclosed for 30 days. About 0.6, so about 0.85%, plus about 0.1% for a state program nobody monitors: A now is about 1%.
  4. The rest. Strict AGI now is 1.5%. C is about a third of it (0.5%), D about a sixth (0.25%), D-open about a fifth (0.3%). B now is about 3% × 0.6, about 2%. The Index is A 1% + about 1.4 points of B outside A + about 0.1 for C or D outside A = 2.5%.

Strict AGI (public or hidden): 1.5% now, 45% by end-2030, 65% by end-2035. Kalshi's "any company announces AGI before Jan 1, 2031" last traded at 83% (82/83, Oct 4, thin); Polymarket's "OpenAI announces AGI before 2027" is at 16% (16/17). Both use looser definitions than ours (our reading).

Rounding: 0.1-point steps below 1%, 0.5-point steps from 1% to 10%, whole points above 10%. Two days of trend progress is far below a rounding step, so the "now" numbers hold.
