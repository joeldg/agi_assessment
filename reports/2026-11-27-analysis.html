Step 4: reasoning discipline

  • A rumour relayed by a credible expert is still a rumour: Aaronson says he is much less sure what the results are, so we treat it as a reason to look, not as evidence of secrecy (our inference).
  • Absence of evidence: we do not treat the lack of any lab comment on cryptography as a sign that results are hidden; that would be unfalsifiable (our inference).
  • Base rates: security-sensitive results are routinely held back for weeks or months under coordinated disclosure, so withholding one would be ordinary and says little about a whole AGI-level system (our inference).
  • AI tools versus AGI: agents running experiments and attack frameworks running with little human involvement are narrow AI used heavily, not an AGI doing the work (our inference).
  • Steelman, it has already happened: labs now publish results that experts expected decades away, and an expert says they hold more, so the true gap could exceed any public metric. Steelman, it hasn't: each result is in one field at a time, and none shows learning on the job or generalization at the v2.0 bar (our inference).
