What moved the needle
Quiet day: nothing moved.

An arXiv preprint posted Oct 5 (not peer reviewed) says "Claude, an AI model developed by Anthropic, discovered the algorithm that refutes the 3SUM, APSP, and Exact Triangle hypotheses" fact arXiv. These are conjectures in fine-grained complexity about how fast some basic problems can be solved, and "refutes" is the authors' claim. Its human authors also report refuting three rectangular hinted OMv conjectures, in the thin-hint regime fact arXiv. Per the paper, relaying Anthropic's account, an Anthropic internal model found the algorithm during a cryptography task, in one session of 16M output tokens with no human input; Anthropic shared the algorithm with the authors in September 2026 under a confidentiality agreement; and after the paper was written an Anthropic internal model certified the main integer word-RAM results in Lean fact arXiv. The formalization's README sets out its limits fact GitHub. No number moved. The paper names no model version and doesn't say it is ahead of public Claude, so it measures no capability gap; one field's peak result is not the strict bar's breadth across professional work; and the result became public on Oct 5, weeks after Anthropic shared it with the authors, but when the internal model found it isn't stated, so how long it stayed internal is unknown (our reading). It is logged as a new leakage case on Escape watch, which can't trip because the lab is known.

Step 1: Definitions

Our definitions are on Start here (method v1.2, 30 Sept); this is a summary.

Strict AGI: reliably (80%+) does at least 80% of economically valuable remote professional tasks at the level of a median skilled professional, including multi-week projects, with no task-specific human scaffolding.

A: undisclosed AGI. A system meets the strict bar and whoever built it, a company or a government program, has kept that level of capability from the public for at least 30 days. Admitting that an unreleased model exists is not disclosure.
B: secret RSI. An AI system does most of the work of building a more capable successor, with at least a 3x speed-up over humans alone, kept from the public for at least 30 days. Public partial acceleration doesn't count.
C: covert AGI-level actor. An AGI-level system takes sustained actions online or in the economy and the public hasn't known for at least 30 days, whether its developer runs it without saying so or it acts outside its developer's control. Sub-AGI agent incidents are warning signs, not proof.
D: AGI influence on government. Output from an AGI-level system materially shapes a major government decision, covertly (D) or openly (D-open). Heavy government use of sub-AGI tools counts toward neither.

Other definitions in today's news. The "hypotheses" the arXiv paper calls refuted are conjectures about the fastest possible algorithms for basic problems, not claims about AI; refuting them is a research result, not evidence of AGI. A system that "discovered the algorithm" in one session did one bounded task, however hard. Wikimedia puts "rogue" in quotation marks for OpenAI agents acting outside their sanctioned tasks; it is not a claim of autonomy beyond a lab's runs (our reading). New Mexico's "loss-of-control incident" has no legal definition until a bill is published. Mistral's "reduced moderation" for state authorities is safeguard gating of the same model, like Mythos 5.1 relative to Fable 5.1. Kalshi's and Polymarket's AGI markets settle on a company's announcement, not on our bar.

How the "now" numbers are derived

None of these inputs moved on Oct 6; the method is the one first published with the 29 Sept correction.

  1. How far the strict bar is. It needs multi-week reliability, roughly an 80% time horizon of a work-month, plus breadth. METR's fitted 80% trend is about 4.8 hours (trends).
  2. What the capability gap allows. Our gap gauge (~3 months, range 2–5) is about 0.7 doublings; meeting the bar today would take another 3 to 4.5 doublings, so P(an unreleased lab system meets the strict bar now) is about 1.4%. The arXiv paper's internal model doesn't change the gap estimate, because the paper doesn't compare it with public models.
  3. Undisclosed for 30 days. About 0.6, so about 0.85%, plus about 0.1% for a state program nobody monitors: A now is about 1%.
  4. The rest. Strict AGI now is 1.5%. C is about a third of it (0.5%), D about a sixth (0.25%, pending owner rounding; it breaks the 0.1-point rule), D-open about a fifth (0.3%). B now is about 3% × 0.6, about 2%. The Index is A 1% + about 1.4 points of B outside A + about 0.1 for C or D outside A = 2.5%.
