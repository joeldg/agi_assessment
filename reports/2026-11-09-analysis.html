  • Learning on the job: An unreviewed preprint, Flex-RSI (arXiv 2611.08946, submitted Nov 6; 22 authors under the byline 'Flex-RSI Team'), sets 24 vision and robotics tasks on which an executor improves a solution and an improver revises the harness, including the executor and itself, under a fixed budget, with final scores on hidden test data. Self-improvement raised the validation score in 150 of 177 runs (85%), by a median of 10.4 points; on a rank-based score, Claude Opus 5.5 is highest (91.6), then Gemini 4 Argon (89.0, on the nine tasks it was run on), GPT-6.1 Sol (77.0) and GPT-6 Astra (65.5); letting the improver edit the harness raised the test score in six of seven paired runs (the authors). (source; expert opinion). Improvement from a system's own work on a task bears on learning on the job, but this is harness self-modification under a fixed budget in an unreviewed preprint, not the part's test of learning (our inference). Pending our weekly check.
  • Reasoning: An unreviewed preprint, 'Discovering formally verified quantum programs with AI' (arXiv 2611.09822, submitted Nov 6), by 25 authors whose only listed affiliations are Google DeepMind and Google Quantum AI, reports teams of LLM agents writing quantum programs in the Lean theorem prover against formal problem specifications, with correctness and cost verified, and improvements on prior baselines of up to 1.4x for parallel search, 290x for quantum arithmetic and 11x, 8x and 2.5x for simulations relevant to fusion, photochemistry and drug metabolism. Researchers and domain experts chose the problems and wrote the specifications and did not steer the work while it ran; the paper names no specific model (the authors). (source; verified fact). Machine-verified discoveries bear on reasoning, but humans chose the problems and wrote the specifications, in one field, and the speedups are unreviewed (our inference). Pending our weekly check.
