Step 1: Definitions

Our definitions are frozen at v1.0 on Start here; this is a summary.

Strict AGI: reliably (80%+) does at least 80% of economically valuable remote professional tasks at the level of a median skilled professional, including multi-week projects, with no task-specific human scaffolding.

A: undisclosed AGI. A system meets the strict bar and whoever built it, a company or a government program, has kept that level of capability from the public for at least 30 days. Admitting that an unreleased model exists is not disclosure.
B: secret RSI. An AI system does most of the work of building a more capable successor, with at least a 3x speed-up over humans alone, undisclosed. Public partial acceleration doesn't count.
C: covert AGI-level actor. An AGI-level system takes sustained actions online or in the economy without its developer's sanction or without public knowledge. Sub-AGI agent incidents are warning signs, not proof.
D: AGI influence on government. Output from an AGI-level system materially shapes a major government decision, covertly (D) or openly (D-open). Heavy government use of sub-AGI tools counts toward neither.

Other definitions in today's news. Z.ai and Extropic both use "recursive self-improvement" for agents doing parts of engineering or research work under human direction; Z.ai itself says it has not reached it. Neither meets B's bar of an AI doing most of the work of building a more capable successor. "Notification" in OpenAI's update means activity met its notification criteria, not that a system was compromised.

  • Unfalsifiability check. "Argon has no model card, so Google must be hiding something" treats a documentation gap in a limited release as evidence of secrecy, when its benchmarks are public and it ranks below a public model. We don't count it.
  • Steelman, "it has already happened." Labs run their newest models internally first, outside evaluations of OpenAI's unreleased model are not public, staff reportedly warned about weak monitoring, and agent incidents keep surfacing months late.

How the "now" numbers are derived

None of these inputs moved on Oct 1–2; the method is the one first published with the 29 Sept correction.

  1. How far the strict bar is. It needs multi-week reliability, roughly an 80% time horizon of a work-month, plus breadth. METR's fitted 80% trend is about 4.7 hours (trends).
  2. What the capability gap allows. Our gap gauge (~3 months, range 2–5) is about 0.7 doublings; meeting the bar today would take another 3 to 4.5 doublings, so P(an unreleased lab system meets the strict bar now) is about 1.4%.
  3. Undisclosed for 30 days. About 0.6, so about 0.85%, plus about 0.1% for a state program nobody monitors: A now is about 1%.
  4. The rest. Strict AGI now is 1.5%. C is about a third of it (0.5%), D about a sixth (0.25%), D-open about a fifth (0.3%). B now is about 3% × 0.6, about 2%. The Index is A 1% + about 1.4 points of B outside A + about 0.1 for C or D outside A = 2.5%.

Strict AGI (public or hidden): 1.5% now, 45% by end-2030, 65% by end-2035. Kalshi's "any company announces AGI before Jan 1, 2031" last traded at 82% (82/83, Oct 2); it uses a looser definition than ours (our reading).

Rounding: 0.1-point steps below 1%, 0.5-point steps from 1% to 10%, whole points above 10%. One day of trend progress is far below a rounding step, so the "now" numbers hold.
